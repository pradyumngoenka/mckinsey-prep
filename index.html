The interview loop

1.
Application screen
McKinsey reviews your CV and application before inviting you to test or interview.
2.
Solve assessment
McKinsey's gamified problem-solving assessment, used for most consulting roles. It tests natural problem-solving; McKinsey says there is no need to prepare.
3.
Personal Experience Interview
A detailed conversation about your real experiences. McKinsey asks for two personal examples per theme, with your specific actions and reasoning, not a rehearsed story.
4.
Problem-solving interview
The case interview: a typical McKinsey client scenario. There is no single right answer; listen carefully and tailor your structure instead of forcing a memorised framework.
5.
Expertise interview
For some roles, an extra conversation about how your skills fit the work. Your recruiter confirms whether it applies.
What is confirmed

Solve and the two interview types are McKinsey's published process for consulting roles. The exact rounds and order for this campus role are confirmed by the recruiter in advance, so treat this loop as the official general shape, not a role-specific schedule.

Two-week prep plan

  1. Days 1-2: read McKinsey's interview guidance end to end. Draft two personal stories for each PEI theme, with specific actions and reasoning.
  2. Days 3-4: work through McKinsey's official practice cases, out loud, untimed first.
  3. Days 5-6: practise market sizing and mental maths daily, 20 minutes. Percentages, growth rates, break-evens.
  4. Days 7-8: brush up growth marketing basics for this role: acquisition, retention, CAC, conversion funnels, and one industry you can discuss.
  5. Days 9-10: two timed mock cases with the live interviewer below, then rework your weakest step.
  6. Days 11-12: two full mock PEI rounds. Record yourself once and listen back for clarity.
  7. Days 13-14: light review only. Prepare questions for your interviewer, test your camera and space, and sleep properly.

Guesstimates

Practice estimates: state your assumptions, keep the arithmetic clean, then sanity-check the answer. Common in consulting and analytics interviews and useful thinking practice for any role. The live interviewer includes one in its case round.

Estimate the cups of chai sold in Kolkata in a day

One illustrative approach: metro population about 15 million. Assume 60% drink chai daily, averaging 1.5 cups each: 15 million x 0.6 x 1.5 is about 13.5 million cups a day. Sanity check: with roughly 30,000 stalls and vendors, that is around 450 cups per seller, plausible for busy areas. Your assumptions can differ; the structure matters more than the exact number.

Estimate the monthly revenue of a neighbourhood coffee shop

One illustrative approach: about 150 orders a day at an average ticket of Rs 250 gives Rs 37,500 a day, so roughly Rs 11 lakh a month. Sanity check: that is about 15 orders an hour over a 10-hour day, reasonable for a busy neighbourhood shop. State your assumptions first, then multiply, then ask whether the result feels right.

AI interviewer prompt (McKinsey version)

Paste this into any free AI chat app, ideally with voice mode on

You are a McKinsey interviewer running a 30-minute mock interview for a Junior Delivery Consultant (Growth Marketing and Sales) role in India. Structure it in three parts. First, one personal experience question: ask me about a time I drove real impact, and probe for my specific actions and reasoning. Second, one case: a consumer brand whose online sales growth has stalled. Let me structure the problem, give me data only when I ask for it, challenge my thinking once, then ask for a recommendation. Third, honest feedback on my structure, communication and maths. Ask one question at a time and wait for my answer before continuing.

On the day

  • Choose a quiet, private space. Headphones help, and keep your charger, pen, paper and water within reach.
  • Keep your camera on and test your video feed beforehand.
  • Switch off AI note-takers and assistants before the interview: McKinsey encourages AI for preparation, but not for generating answers during interviews or assessments.
  • If a question is not clear, ask the interviewer to repeat it.
